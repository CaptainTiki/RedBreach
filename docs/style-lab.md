# Style lab S-01: Phase 1

Status: built and validated, 2026-09-25, awaiting the user's walkthrough. This
is Phase 1 of the [clean-slate test plan](clean-slate-test-plan.md). It is the R-01 retro industrial sample (never built, since removed)
extended to three profiles, two texture directions, three light rigs and a Mars window.

**Walk it:** press **F9** from any gym or lab.

| Key | Switches |
|---|---|
| **T** | Texture set A (Level Eleven concrete and steel) / B (QUOD tan and olive). Same geometry. |
| **L** | Corridor light rig: ceiling centre, ceiling edges, or low wash |
| **P** | Presentation layer on / off (volumetric fog, glow, ACES grade, SSAO) |

The top-left label shows the current state.

## Reference board

The user's favourites, saved in `docs/references/style-lab/`:

| Reference | What it does | What we took from it |
|---|---|---|
| ShotHop, lava room | One saturated hue floods the room; the lava is the light source | An emissive hazard that lights its surroundings: the coolant pool |
| ShotHop, truss hall | Crunchy concrete, heavy columns, diagonal steel trusses, a catwalk rail, one accent (a red laser door) against neutral greys | Big structural members; neutral materials with a single accent |
| STRAFE, toxic catwalks | Chunky panelled walls, low tiled ceilings, catwalks over glowing toxic liquid, fire as a warm accent | Glowing liquid below a walkway; panel texture carries the detail |

Common to all three: **low-res textures at a Quake-like density, one or two
saturated emissive accents against a neutral base, large simple structural
forms, and a colour grade.** None of them depend on fine geometry.

## What is built

One straight walk: start door, **P1**, bulkhead, **P2**, bulkhead, **P3**,
portal, then the machine room. Each section is 12 m long with ribs at a 4 m
pitch (three bays). Floors stay at 0 m.

| Profile | Section | Clear at 1.8 m | Between ribs at 1.8 m |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P1 full trapezoid** | 6 m floor, 3 m ceiling, 4 m high. The whole wall leans in. | 4.65 m | 3.90 m |
| **P2 knee-wall trapezoid** | Vertical to 2 m, then slopes in to a 3.5 m ceiling | 6.00 m | 5.25 m |
| **P3 clipped box** | Vertical to 3 m, then a 45° chamfer to a 4 m ceiling | 6.00 m | 5.25 m |

- **Plinth band:** 1 m high and 0.125 m proud, so every texture change sits on
  real geometry. On P1 it is a parallel sloped facet that leaves a 0.125 m ledge.
- **Bulkheads:** 1 m thick frames with a 4 × 3.25 m chamfered opening and a
  hazard reveal. The one into the room (B3) stands 0.25 m proud of the room wall.
- **Machine room:** 16 × 16 m octagon with 2 m clipped corners. Walls rise to
  4.5 m, then a 45° haunch runs up to a 6 m ceiling. In the centre, a
  curb-lipped plinth holds a glowing coolant pool and an octagonal tank with
  glow slots, two risers, an overhead pipe run and two control desks.
- **Mars window:** 6 × 2.5 m, glazed with a clip brush. Outside is regolith,
  eight rocks and a mesa with a talus apron, under a procedural butterscotch sky
  lit by a low warm sun.

### Texture roles: one map, any texture set

Every face names a **role** (`role/floor`, `role/rib`, `role/hazard`, and so
on; 19 roles). Each texture set supplies one material per role in
`textures/style_a/role/` or `textures/style_b/role/`. The map settings choose
the set.

The same map is built twice, once per set. Set B keeps its visuals only, so
its collision never doubles up. This **proves the role-swap mechanism** the
plan proposed: changing texture direction is a settings change, not a
retexture.

The reason it works: func_godot derives UVs from the role material's albedo
size, so each set lands at its intended density.

- **Density standard: 32 px/m**, which is Quake's (at 32 map units per metre,
  scale 1.0 means one texel per unit). A 64 px texture spans 2 m.
- **Exceptions:** ribs, frames and hazard sample at 64 px/m. At 32 px/m the
  hazard stripes were 2 m wide and too loud.
- **Pack textures import lossless** (no VRAM compression), so the pixels stay
  crisp.
- **Filtering:** nearest with anisotropic mipmaps.
- **TrenchBroom previews** of the roles are in `textures/role/`, copied from
  set A.

The Mars ground and rock are this lab's first **bespoke textures**: 64 px,
generated by `write-style-lab-scene.py` with the standard library only.

## Measured: question C1, lighting a wall that leans in

The worry was that inward-leaning walls face partly downward, so ceiling
lights would graze them and leave them dark. **The measurement says otherwise.**

The fittings sit 0.5 m below the ceiling, and the slope faces the corridor
centre. A fitting on the centreline therefore sits above and inward of most of
the slope and lights it well. Measured on set A with the presentation layer on:

| Wall views (centre-right region) | Ceiling centre | Ceiling edges | Low wash |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| P1 upper wall | 87.5 | **98.0** | 50.2 |
| P2 upper wall | 85.3 | **96.8** | 48.3 |
| P3 upper wall | 87.5 | **99.4** | 47.2 |

| Down-corridor views | Ceiling | Walls | Floor |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1: centre / edges / low | 63.5 / 67.2 / 33.2 | 88.8 / 86.8 / **106.2** | 17.5 / 18.2 / 12.2 |
| P3: centre / edges / low | 55.3 / 55.7 / 31.2 | 89.0 / 86.3 / 53.5 | 18.1 / 17.1 / 7.9 |

Mean perceived brightness, 0–255.

- **Ceiling edges** lights the upper walls about 11 points brighter than
  centre, at the same ceiling brightness.
- **Low wash** is the moody option. It lights the lower walls and leaves the
  ceiling at half brightness. On P1 it gives the brightest walls of all,
  because a light at 1.35 m faces the downward-leaning slope squarely. It is
  much weaker on P3's vertical walls.

**The trapezoid needs no special lighting.** Choosing a rig is a mood
decision, not a fix.

## Build and validation

  The map is grouped in TrenchBroom by section, bulkhead and room part.
- **`tools/rebuild-style-lab.ps1 -Validate -Capture`** re-imports textures,
  builds both sets, then runs **115 checks with 0 failures**:
  - brush count against collision count
  - no surface falling back to the default texture, in either set
  - a standing capsule clear at the centre and ±1.5 m, every 0.5 m, through all
    three profiles
  - headroom in every bay

  - lane-edge walks

  - the window is glazed

### Source files

- `tools/style_lab.py` is the single source of truth.
- `tools/brushkit.py` is the new **reusable brush writer**. It builds any
  convex hull, has role callbacks per face, and gives world-anchored unit
  tangent UVs on slopes, with a V anchor for pinning painted bands.
- `tools/bootstrap-style-lab.py` writes the map. It is one-time and refuses to
  overwrite.
- `tools/write-style-lab-scene.py` writes the materials, previews, lossless
  imports, map settings, the scene and `style/style_lab_data.json`. The
  capture and validation scripts read their numbers from that JSON.

## Production data (plan question E1)

- **About 20 minutes of Claude time** from the user's go-ahead (17:46) to the
  first validated capture (about 18:06). That covered reading references,
  installing packs, writing brushkit, the lab, both texture sets, the scene,
  validation and captures. Tuning and documentation followed.
- **Caveats:** it reused an existing pipeline and a well-understood corridor
  language. A real level has layout, progression and encounter work this lab
  does not. Treat it as a floor for room authoring time, not a level estimate.
- **Joins (question B1):** there was **no special-case fill geometry**. Two
  choices did the work. **Bulkheads join any two profiles**: a thick frame hides
  a profile change completely, which is cheaper than the T-jamb problem and
  also reads as a pressure compartment. And **convex-hull brushes with mitred
  ends** made the octagon room, its haunches and window cuts straightforward.

## Claude's first read (for the user to overrule)

- **P1, the full trapezoid, is the most distinctive silhouette.** P2 and P3
  read as close cousins at player height. If the style needs one signature
  corridor, P1 is it, with P3 as the plain workhorse.
- **Set A** reads cold and hard: an engineered colony. **Set B** reads warm and
  retro, closer to QUOD, and its teal floor pairs with the coolant.
- **The machine room and the window** are the postcard candidates: the
  emissive coolant against the neutral room, and the warm Mars light through
  the glass.
- **Known weak spots:**
  - set A's framed floor plate is busy
  - the room's general light is flat
  - the desks are plain boxes
  - the captures include the gameplay HUD

## User walkthrough 01, 2026-09-25

- **P1, the full trapezoid, is the favourite:** "a pretty cool looking
  trapezoidal corridor". It is the signature profile.
- **P2, the knee-wall trapezoid, is the next best.** The profiles are **not
  exclusive**: use P1 and P2 in different places. P3 was not picked. It stays
  available but is not a priority for the kit.
- **Textures:** "looking good so far". A vs B, the light rig and the
  presentation layer are still open.

### Follow-up feedback and revision 02, 2026-09-25

The user's notes:
- **Texture sets:** no need to pick one. They prefer the QUOD pack's textures,
  but red outside and red inside is one note, so the level needs metal and
  concrete colours too. The warm red/yellow can be the look of certain sections.
- **Set A's floor** is too busy.
- **Lighting:** likes it as is. Ceiling edges plus wall lights fit the corridor
  better than the "common" centre strip. They also want to see floor lighting
  and an unlit portion, to learn what can be done with broken or damaged areas.
- **Hazard stripes:** "really dislike" the caution texture over large areas.
- **Presentation layer:** couldn't tell whether they like it or whether it is
  too subtle to see.

Revision 02, built. 163 brushes; **306 checks with 0 failures**; sheets
recaptured:
[palettes](style-lab-palettes.png), [lighting](style-lab-lighting.png),
[presentation](style-lab-presentation.png).

**Looks and zones replace texture sets.** A face names `looks/<look>/<role>`.
There are three looks:
- **warm**: QUOD tan, olive and rust.
- **steel**: Level Eleven grey metal with QUOD grey doors and ceiling.
- **concrete**: pale concrete with a muted green band and a dark plate floor.
  The blue band was tried first and was garish.

The source map carries the **zoned mix**: P1 steel, P2 warm, P3 concrete,
room steel, machine warm, and a continuous warm teal floor. Bulkheads are the
zone boundaries, so every change of look sits on geometry.

**T cycles palettes.** Each palette is a build-time remap
(`looks/warm/` → `looks/steel/`, and so on) of a copy of the one source map,
built visual-only. It is never a second map. The validator checks that each
single-look palette wears only its look.

**Hazard is a trim, not a surface.** Bulkhead reveals are now the metal frame
texture. Thin 0.25 m hazard trims appear only round the machine-room portal,
and the machine plinth sides are dark; only the curb lip is striped.

**Lighting runs as tiers per bay and plans across the walk. L cycles plans:**
- **mixed** (default): P1 uses ceiling edges plus wall lights, P2 is
  floor-lit, and P3 goes edge plus wall, then a failing fitting (seeded
  flicker with spark bursts), then a dead bay under a red emergency beacon.
- **damaged**: a flicker in P1, P2 dead under emergency red, P3 dead with no
  light at all.
- **edge + wall**, **floor**, and **ceiling centre** (revision 01) everywhere.

Fittings and their lights always switch together, and a dead fitting never
glows. Floor lighting needed 1.0 energy with each light 0.7 m off the wall.
At the first setting it lit almost nothing. Measured: P2 walls 135, P1 walls
96 under floor light alone. The dead bay reads about 1 on the floor and 30 on
the walls, against 50 and 120 lit.

**P cycles presentation: off, subtle (the revision 01 layer) and strong.**
Strong is thicker volumetric fog, a larger glow and a stronger grade, so the
effect can be judged by contrast.

### Feedback on revision 02, and revision 03, 2026-09-25

The user's notes on revision 02:
- **The zoned mix** of textures is working well.
- **The unlit versions** are cool, dark and moody.
- **The rib texture** (Level Eleven "Trims") reads as broken on a pillar
  because of its vertical lines.
- **The fog** is mostly liked, but it is yellow. Dusty yellow suits the
  outside of the base; inside it should be a dark colour.
- **Bloom on the machine:** some is good. In the steel and concrete looks it
  was too much and "looks like it's gone nuclear".

Revision 03, built. 306 checks, 0 failures; sheets recaptured.

- **Structure is solid.** Ribs and frames use plain panels in every look:
  steel dark plain metal, concrete plain grey with dark frames, warm olive.
  **Rule: a structural member reads as one piece, so no lined trim textures on
  pillars or frames.**
- **The machine is calm.** The machine body is a dark ribbed panel in every
  look. The pale one caught the coolant light and bloomed. Coolant emission
  dropped from 2.2 to 1.5 and its lights from 1.3 to 0.9, and glow is lower in
  both presentation levels (subtle: intensity 0.7, HDR threshold 1.1).
- **Fog is split: dark neutral inside, warm dust outside.** The global
  volumetric fog is now neutral blue-grey (0.45, 0.48, 0.52), with almost no
  ambient injection, so indoors it only makes halos round the lights and
  never glows yellow. A box `FogVolume` (`MarsDust`) covers only the exterior,
  starting just outside the window glass, with warm dust at density 0.008 and
  a height falloff. Its first density, 0.035, turned the view into a flat
  orange wall. A warm distance fog beginning at 60 m softens the mesa and
  never reaches an interior view.

### Phase 1 settled, 2026-09-25

The user's verdict on revision 03: **"Everything else I think we've got it!"**
The one critique was of the Mars view across the three presentation levels:
off has a little haze, **subtle is perfect**, and strong is too heavy.

The style sheet that carries into Phase 2:

| Decision | Settled value |
|---|---|
| Signature corridor | **P1, the full trapezoid.** P2, the knee wall, is secondary; use both anywhere. P3 is not in the kit. |
| Textures | Zoned **looks** (warm QUOD, steel, concrete), changing only at bulkheads or other geometry. Crunchy 32 px/m with nearest filtering. Floors stay calm. |
| Structure | Ribs and frames use plain panels: one solid member, never lined trim. |
| Hazard | Thin trim only, never a surface. |
| Corridor light | Ceiling-edge strips plus wall lights. Floor lighting for chosen areas. Damage = flicker with sparks, dead bays, red emergency beacons. |
| Emissives | A little bloom; dark bodies round glowing parts. |
| Presentation | **Subtle** is the standard: dark neutral volumetric fog inside, thin warm dust (a FogVolume) outside, ACES grade, modest glow, SSAO. Strong is too heavy and is kept only as a comparison. |

## Questions for the walkthrough (answered; kept for the record)
